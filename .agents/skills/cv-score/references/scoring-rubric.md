# CV scoring rubric

Use integer component scores and show the arithmetic. Do not choose a final number first and reverse-engineer the explanation.

## ATS readiness — 100 points

| Component | Points | What earns full credit |
|---|---:|---|
| Must-have qualification coverage | 30 | Every material requirement is represented accurately or by a clear equivalent |
| Technical and domain keyword coverage | 25 | Important technologies and domain terms appear naturally in evidenced context |
| Responsibility alignment | 15 | Bullets demonstrate the job's central work rather than only adjacent experience |
| Evidence and achievement quality | 15 | Claims show concrete contribution, mechanism, and supported results or scale |
| Structure and parseability | 10 | Conventional sections, readable LaTeX/PDF text, consistent dates, and scan-friendly bullets |
| Consistency and error control | 5 | No contact errors, contradictions, unexplained ambiguity, or careless language |

## Hiring-manager fit — 100 points

| Component | Points | What earns full credit |
|---|---:|---|
| Role relevance | 30 | The most visible evidence closely matches the role's problems and priorities |
| Demonstrated impact | 25 | Achievements communicate credible outcomes, scale, quality, or user/business value |
| Technical depth | 20 | Bullets explain meaningful engineering mechanisms and decisions |
| Ownership and scope | 15 | The candidate's contribution and level of responsibility are clear and credible |
| Skimmability and differentiation | 10 | Strong evidence is front-loaded, concise, non-repetitive, and easy to find |

## Score anchors

- **90–100:** unusually strong alignment with no material presentation or evidence gap.
- **80–89:** competitive alignment with a few fixable omissions or weaker areas.
- **70–79:** plausible candidate, but several important requirements or signals are missing.
- **60–69:** partial fit; substantial tailoring or stronger evidence is needed.
- **Below 60:** major qualification, relevance, credibility, or presentation gaps.

A score above 90 should be rare. Do not award points for repeated keywords without evidence or deduct twice for the same underlying weakness unless it independently affects two components.

## Shared requirement decisions

Use the priority, category, coverage, evidence, placement, role-lens, and frozen-comparison definitions in `../../../references/cv-requirement-rules.md`. Do not introduce a separate keyword taxonomy or frequency target in the scoring rubric.

## Content regressions

- A keyword gain does not offset the loss of a stronger supported metric, outcome, ownership signal, mechanism, domain fact, or differentiating achievement.
- Charge a genuine loss to the component it weakens, such as evidence quality, demonstrated impact, technical depth, ownership, or skimmability. Do not deduct twice when one regression affects two descriptions of the same underlying weakness.
- On a comparable rescore, identify regressions separately even when the total score rises.

## Parseability confidence

- Source-only review can assess conventional structure and extractable LaTeX text but cannot prove the submitted PDF's reading order.
- Award high parseability confidence only when an explicitly authorised or locally generated PDF was text-extracted and its critical content and order were verified.
- Treat a skipped rendered check as an evidence limitation, not an automatic formatting failure.

## Interview likelihood

Use a range, label, confidence, and assumptions rather than a false single-point probability. Consider seniority, location/work eligibility only when stated, applicant competition uncertainty, must-have gaps, and the two scores. Never imply access to employer conversion data.
